ai-or-die × Happy — recommended path forward

Question: can we drive our ai-or-die Claude sessions from the existing Happy iPhone/iPad app, keeping app modification as a last resort — and does its security model allow it?

Yes. Use the Happy app unmodified. No fork required.
Happy ships a custom-server-URL setting (no rebuild), it's end-to-end encrypted (the relay it talks to is untrusted by design), and it has no cert pinning or host lock — so it works over a private Tailscale/LAN endpoint that is never exposed to the internet. Everything we build lives on our side (a relay + a producer adapter); we never touch the app.

Why the security model allows "app as-is"

End-to-end encrypted

The relay only ever holds ciphertext + routing metadata. Even though we run the relay, session content is encrypted with a key the app owns (delivered once via QR pairing). Threat model = access/abuse, not confidentiality.

No open endpoint needed

App has no embedded VPN/mesh and no cert pinning (source-audited). Relay lives inside your tailnet/LAN; the OS routes the app to https://host.ts.net transparently. Want no Tailscale on the phone? tailscale funnel publishes just that port (HTTPS+wss, valid cert), gated by E2E + a pubkey allowlist.

Single-user lockdown

Registration is by public key; we add a one-line ALLOWED_ACCOUNT_PUBKEYS allowlist so only your device's key can pair. Google can still gate access — as Tailscale's SSO, at the network layer.

Nothing to maintain in the app

No app fork means no App-Store account, no rebuilds chasing Expo/RN upgrades, no keeping a fork in sync with upstream. That burden is exactly what "last resort" is meant to avoid.

Residual risks & mitigations

  • verify Push notifications. Expo tokens are bound to Happy's project; a self-host relay forwards to Expo (happy-cli does this). Likely works, to confirm empirically. Foreground realtime needs no push.
  • fidelity Permission approvals. The app expects an RPC lifecycle + a cleared request; ai-or-die drives a real PTY. We map the modals it reliably detects and clear state proactively; edge permission shapes may not be 1:1.
  • churn Pre-1.0 protocol. Emit legacy behind a mapper; pin behavior with tests against the real app build (the app validates with its own copy of the types).
  • win Windows-first. ConPTY prompt parsing for the permission path must be tested on Windows.

The only scenario that could ever force an app fork is if push turned out to strictly require our own Expo project — and even then, live in-app use works without it. Nothing else in the audit points to a fork.
